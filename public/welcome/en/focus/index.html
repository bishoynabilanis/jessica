Twenty-five minutes is the whole trick

📖 3 minute read · updated November 2026

Most students do not study for hours — they study in broken minutes. The focus timer splits the work into short sessions: twenty-five minutes of study, five minutes of rest, and the minutes that pass are written into your plan without you tracking anything.

Why 25 minutes How it works Where the minutes go Questions

Why a short session beats “I will study for two hours”

A long block makes the brain postpone the start, and every postponed start brings the exam closer. A short session removes the wall: twenty-five minutes in front of you, then a rest you have earned. And once you start, the hardest part is already behind you. In Jessica the timer is not a standalone gadget — it is tied to the plan, so the session you finish shows up as real progress.

  • An easy start: a small goal that actually begins, instead of a big plan that waits.
  • A counted break: five minutes after every session, because studying without rest turns understanding into memorising.
  • A number that moves: the minutes become XP and appear in the weekly review, so you see the effect of each session.

How it works — the three things you need

1) Pick a lengthFifteen minutes for primary and quick revision, twenty-five for preparatory, fifty for secondary and the final revision.
2) Press startA ring counts down in large digits. You can pause at any moment and resume later, and the timer keeps counting even when the tab is in the background.
3) Take the breakThe five-minute button gives you a real break. A short chime plays at the end and the screen stays awake while the session runs.

If the page reloads by accident or the browser closes, the session comes back exactly as it was, because the remaining time is calculated from an end time rather than a counter ticking in the background.

Where the minutes end up

Every focus session is recorded automatically: minutes in the day's log, XP (sixty minutes is one point), and attendance in the daily plan. After a week the plan page shows a weekly review with the total minutes, the days you actually studied and your answer accuracy — plus the moves for the week after. That number is what stops a plan from being words on a page.

Try one twenty-five minute session now — it will finish before you notice.

Frequently asked

Where do the minutes go?

Every minute is written into the daily plan and the study log, and each sixty minutes becomes XP. The session shows up in the weekly review and on your progress map.

Does the timer keep running in the background?

Yes. The timer counts from an end time, so it keeps going when the tab is in the background or the page reloads, and you come back to the right remaining time.

What length suits a child?

Fifteen minutes is enough for primary, twenty-five suits preparatory, and fifty is for secondary or exam revision — always followed by the five-minute break.

Does it need internet?

No. The timer runs entirely on the device, with no account and no key.
